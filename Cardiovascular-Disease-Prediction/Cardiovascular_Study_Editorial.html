ABDALLAH MESTRAH03 / 12

Fix contradictions. Limit extremes.

We treat contradictory records separately from outliers. The pressure rule removes 1,258 rows, leaving 68,742. For the remaining data, the selected pipeline clips four inputs. The IQR rule is Q1 minus 1.5 times IQR and Q3 plus 1.5 times IQR. For the final model, a systolic reading of 200 is capped at 170. During validation, each training fold learns its own caps. The notebook also compared keeping and deleting outliers; the score differences were small.

ABDALLAH MESTRAH06 / 12

Small trees. Sequential corrections.

Gradient boosting adds trees in sequence, with each new tree learning corrections. The histogram method bins numeric values to make searching for splits faster. The selected settings are 200 iterations, learning rate 0.03, 15 maximum leaves and 20 minimum samples per leaf. At the end, the probability is compared with 0.50. Tuning improved ROC-AUC modestly but not every metric: validation disease recall changed from 69.12 to 68.86 percent. The updated decision cutoff is 0.37; the historical tuning scores used 0.50.

ABDALLAH MESTRAH08 / 12

What changes at threshold 0.37?

Lowering the cutoff means a record needs a smaller disease score to be classified as disease. This isolated comparison uses the same saved out-of-fold probabilities for 54,994 development records. Disease recall increases from 68.86 to 81.18 percent, but no-disease recall falls to 61.63 percent. We detect 3,360 additional disease cases and create 4,513 additional false alarms. Accuracy falls to 71.33 percent and disease precision to 67.57 percent. These are validation results, not the test scores on the previous slide. The trained trees are unchanged; the chosen decision cutoff is now 0.37. ROC-AUC is unchanged because the probabilities are unchanged.

ABDALLAH MESTRAH09 / 12

Why choose 0.37 instead of 0.35?

We chose 0.37 over 0.35 to reduce false alarms while keeping validation disease recall above 80 percent. Compared with 0.35, it produces 1,163 fewer false alarms but misses 686 more disease cases. Accuracy is 71.33 percent. This is a development-data decision, not independent test confirmation.
